[16] 2026 · Preprint

Limit theorems for random walks with spatio-temporal drift

(with Ngo P. N. Ngoc)

Keywords: random walks with spatio-temporal drift, random walks with asymptotically zero drift, multi-dimensional random walks, limit theorems

arXiv:2605.17725 · PDF

Abstract

We consider discrete-time walks in \(\mathbb R^d\) with a conditional drift that decreases polynomially with time and increases polynomially with distance from the origin. These models are connected to self-interacting random processes. Assuming that the increments admit a moment of some order \(p>2\), we investigate the limiting behaviour. When the drift is linear in the position, a phase transition occurs among Gaussian distributional limits of the normalized walk. For nonlinear drift, a critical line separates three regimes: a Gaussian limit, a non-Gaussian limit described by the stationary law of a stochastic differential equation, and almost sure localization of the normalized walk on a hypersphere.

[15] 2026 · Preprint

"True" self-avoiding walks on general trees

Keywords: branching-ruin number, phase transition, true self-avoiding walks, random walks on trees, polynomial trees

arXiv:2604.24389 · PDF

Abstract

We investigate true self-avoiding walks on infinite locally finite trees. Starting at the root, the walk chooses an incident edge at each step with probability proportional to its weight; an edge traversed \(n\) times has weight \(w(n)=\exp(-\beta n)\). We establish a sharp recurrence–transience transition in terms of the branching-ruin number of the tree, which is also the Hausdorff dimension of its boundary for a suitable metric. Transience holds almost surely when this number exceeds \(1/2\), and recurrence holds when it is smaller than \(1/2\). The result answers a question of Kosygina.

Abstract

[13] 2025

The secretary problem with strategic secretaries

(with Jack Mills)

2025 MATRIX Annals Part I.

2025 MATRIX Annals Part I · PDF

Abstract

We study a novel variant of the secretary problem. In the original secretary problem, a committee is shown a sequence of secretaries and tasked with stopping the interviews at the best secretary. Since the sequence of secretaries is random and the committee cannot look into the future, the solution to this problem is an optimal stopping rule that maximises the probability of selecting the best secretary. In the variant introduced in this paper, one or some of the secretaries are given agency in the position they are interviewed. This results in a game where the committee is an agent still aiming to maximise the probability of hiring the best secretary and the secretaries are all aiming to maximise their probability of being hired. The main results include an optimal strategy for the strategic secretary that is non-monotonic in the number of secretaries, as well as an asymptotic formula describing the optimal strategy for strategic secretaries of any rank.

Strongly vertex-reinforced jump process on a graph with bounded degree

(with Andrea Collevecchio)

Keywords: self-interacting processes, random processes with reinforcement, vertex-reinforced jump processes, localization

arXiv:2401.04366 · PDF

Abstract

We study nonlinear vertex-reinforced jump processes on arbitrary infinite graphs of bounded degree. Suppose that the reinforcement function \(w\) is nondecreasing and that its reciprocal is integrable. We prove that almost surely only finitely many vertices are visited. When \(w\) is comparable to a superlinear polynomial, the process ultimately remains on a star-shaped subgraph and exactly one vertex has unbounded local time.

Finding pure Nash equilibria in large random games

(with Andrea Collevecchio and Ziwen Zhong)

To appear in Mathematics of Operations Research.

Keywords: random games, pure Nash equilibria, best-response dynamics, percolation, random walks on an oriented hypercube

arXiv:2406.09732 · PDF

Abstract

Best response dynamics updates a strategy profile by randomly selecting players who can improve their payoff by changing strategy. A pure Nash equilibrium is an absorbing profile, but an equilibrium need not exist and the dynamics may enter a subgame without one. We investigate random games with \(N\) players, two actions per player, and independent identically distributed payoffs, allowing distributions with atoms and hence ties. The game defines a random environment on the \(N\)-dimensional hypercube, in which pure Nash equilibria and traps are distinguished sets. We prove that a class of walks, including best response dynamics and simple random walk, reaches an equilibrium before entering any trap with high probability.

[10] 2022 · Published

Vertex-reinforced jump process on the integers with nonlinear reinforcement

(with Andrea Collevecchio and Stanislav Volkov)

Annals of Applied Probability. Volume 32 (2022), Issue 4, p. 2671–2705.

Keywords: localization, random processes with reinforcement, Self-interacting processes, vertex-reinforced jump processes

arXiv:2004.05927 · PDF · Journal / DOI

Abstract

We consider a nonlinear vertex-reinforced jump process on \(\mathbb Z\) with initial weights one and an increasing measurable weight function \(w:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)\). If \(\int_1^\infty du/w(u)=\infty\), we prove recurrence, with every vertex visited infinitely often and all local times unbounded. If \(\int_1^\infty du/w(u)<\infty\) and, for some \(\rho>0\), the function \(t\mapsto w(t)^\rho\int_t^\infty du/w(u)\) is nonincreasing, we prove eventual localization on precisely three vertices, exactly one of which has unbounded local time. For equal initial weights, transience is impossible: some vertex receives infinitely many visits. This extends the recurrence result of Davis and Volkov (2002) for linear reinforcement.

[9] 2022 · Published

Speed of excited random walks with long backward steps

Journal of Statistical Physics. Volume 188 (2022), Issue 1, Article no. 3.

Keywords: Excited random walks, Non-nearest neighbour random walks, Multi-type branching processes with emigration

arXiv:2103.10610 · PDF · Journal / DOI

Abstract

We study multi-excited walks on \(\mathbb Z\) whose jumps from \(x\) are either to \(x+1\) or to \(x-i\), where \(1\le i\le L\) and \(L\ge1\). We identify an associated multitype branching structure and prove a limit theorem for a multitype Galton–Watson process with emigration. Combining this theorem with the method of Basdevant and Singh (2008), we extend their nearest-neighbour speed criterion: in the regime of right transience, the speed is strictly positive exactly when the expected total drift \(\delta\) exceeds two. This establishes a special case of a conjecture of Davis and Peterson.

On a class of random walks in simplexes

(with Stanislav Volkov)

Journal of Applied Probability. Volume 57 (2020), Issue 2, p. 409–428.

Keywords: Random walks in simplexes, iterated random functions, Dirichlet distribution, stick-breaking process

arXiv:1709.00174 · PDF · Journal / DOI

Abstract

We consider Markov chains in the standard simplex of dimension \(d\ge1\). From an interior point, the chain selects one of the \(d+1\) vertices with probabilities depending on its current position, and jumps to a random point on the segment towards the selected vertex. Using properties of beta distributions, we identify Dirichlet limiting laws in several cases. We also investigate an urn-based history-dependent walk on \([0,1]\) and prove convergence in distribution to the arcsine law.

A universal result for consecutive random subdivision of polygons

(with Stanislav Volkov)

Random Structures & Algorithms. Volume 51 (2017), Issue 2, p. 341–371.

Keywords: Random subdivisions, products of random matrices, Lyapunov exponents

arXiv:1506.04942 · PDF · Journal / DOI

Abstract

Starting from a convex polygon with \(d\ge3\) edges, we independently choose a point on each edge according to a common distribution of the relative position, denoted by \(\xi\). Connecting these points and repeating the procedure defines a sequence of random polygons. Under mild nondegeneracy assumptions on \(\xi\), we prove that their shapes become asymptotically flat. We study the rate of convergence and, for triangles, express how the exact rate can be calculated through Lyapunov exponents. Using products of random matrices, we extend the results of Volkov (2013).
