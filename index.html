About Me

My name is Tuan-Minh Nguyen (Nguyễn Tuấn Minh in Vietnamese), also known as Minh Nguyen. My surname Nguyen is pronounced approximately /ŋwien/ or /ŋuen/. I am a research fellow in probability theory at the School of Mathematics, Monash University, Australia.

I was born in Hue, Vietnam. Before moving to Australia, I completed my doctorate at Lund University in Sweden, supervised by Professor Stanislav Volkov. I received my undergraduate diploma from Belarusian State University.

Research Interests

Probability theory is my primary area of research. I have been working on random processes on graphs, particularly self-interacting random processes. These are natural stochastic models whose future behavior depends not only on their current state but also on their history and surrounding environment. Typical examples include:

  • random walks with reinforcement: the walker prefers returning to previously visited sites rather than exploring unfamiliar ones. See my papers on the localisation of vertex-reinforced jump processes: [RN2021], [CNV2022] and [CN2024].
  • random walks with repulsion: the walker prefers exploring new sites rather than returning to previously visited ones. See my recent paper on the phase transition between transience and recurrence for “true” self-avoiding walks on trees.
  • excited random walks: the walker's behavior depends on the number of visits to its current position. See my papers [LN2026] on the phase transition between transience and recurrence for once-excited random walks on trees; [N2022] and [CHN2021] on the speed of excited random walks with long-range jumps on the integers.
  • random walks in random environments: the walker’s behavior is influenced by its surrounding environment, where the environments at different sites may be independent or strongly correlated. In my recent paper [N2026], I proved the square-logarithmic superdiffusivity of random walks in the randomly oriented Manhattan lattice. See also [CNZ2024] for a result on random walks in randomly oriented hypercubes, with an application to finding pure Nash equilibria in random games.

I am also interested in random graphs, percolation theory, branching processes, random games, random matrices, queueing theory, point processes, SPDE, Malliavin calculus and related problems.
